## Reading it

- **On the tracebacks, the tools take the model from 18.8% to 86.4%,** level with DepTime's own first run. It
  called a tool on 188 of 191 questions without being told to (`explain_error` 136 times, `symbol_history` 122). Of
  the 181 tracebacks DepTime by itself answers right now, it gets 163 right (90.1%), and it gets 2 of the 10 DepTime
  misses. 5 replies were empty.
- **On the upgrade checks the tools help a lot but the model loses much of DepTime's precision.** It finds 82.2% of
  breaks (from 30.9%) and flags 4.5% of working uses (from 15.4%), where DepTime by itself finds 96.7% and flags
  0.1%. As a check on pull requests it would still fail one clean update in six.
- **Where the upgrade answers go wrong:**
  - 77 of its 96 false alarms are uses DepTime by itself reads as working, and 17 are ones it answers `unknown`;
    the model called `check_symbols` in the conversations behind 89 of them.
  - One cause is the question's own notation: in 53 of 315 `check_symbols` calls (38 conversations) the model copied
    the question's `name=` and `_` into the keyword names, which the tool then rejects as unknown keywords. 39 of
    the 96 false alarms are in those conversations. Code in a real project has no such notation, so this part of
    the gap is likely an artefact of how the benchmark writes a use; it wasn't changed after the run.
  - The 27 breaks it misses are all answered `unknown` or left without a verdict, never `works`: 26 of them are
    ones DepTime by itself flags, half in conversations where it never called `check_symbols`.
- **So the tool is most useful to a small model where it gives one direct answer** (`explain_error` on a
  traceback), and less where the model has to combine many answers itself (a verdict per use from several calls).
