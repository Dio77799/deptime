# A model alone on the holdout tracebacks: results (DT-QA-16)

Run once, on 2026-11-14, under the protocol in [README.md](README.md), which was committed (`29acf87`) before any
model was asked. The ten-case trial showed nothing to change in the prompt or the rule, so its replies are the
first ten of run 1. All 1,719 calls (191 tracebacks, three models, three runs) came back as one JSON object in the
fields asked for; none failed or was asked twice.

## Reading it

- **On the headline, the two larger models are close to DepTime.** DepTime's first runs have 166 of 191; Claude
  Opus 5.5 has 159 or 160 and Claude Sonnet 5.5 156 to 158, from memory, with no tools. Where DepTime and Opus
  disagree, DepTime has 28 cases and Opus 21, which is not a difference 191 cases can establish (DepTime's own 95%
  interval is 81.4% to 91.0%). Claude Haiku 5.5 is 18 to 21 points lower.
- **The models are better at naming what changed, and much better at the replacement.** Opus and Sonnet name the
  right package, name and kind of change for 98% to 100% of the tracebacks, where DepTime's first runs have 87.4%:
  DepTime gave no finding for 15 of the 191 (a traceback shape it doesn't parse, a name its index can't follow) and
  a wrong one for 9. The right replacement is in the models' first three for 87% to 99% of the 85 cases that have
  one, and in DepTime's for 48%.
- **DepTime is better at the release, wherever the question doesn't give it away.** Nearly every miss of the larger
  models is a wrong release (29 to 32 a run, against 0 to 3 of any other kind). The table "Where the installed
  release is the boundary" shows where: in 132 cases the installed release is itself the first without the name, so
  "it went in the release you have" is right without knowing the library's history, and there Opus and Sonnet have
  89% to 92% against DepTime's 86.4%. In the other 59 the boundary lies further back, or ahead of the installed
  release, and the models have 63% to 66% (Haiku 46% to 49%) against DepTime's 88.1%. Names not yet added are the
  clearest case: DepTime 96.7%, the models 43% to 63%.
- **The training cutoff shows no drop, for the same reason.** The models do better on the 22 breaks released after
  June 2026 than on the earlier ones. In 21 of those 22 the installed release is the boundary, so they are among
  the cases a reader can answer from the question. This split doesn't measure what it was meant to; the split by
  where the boundary lies does.
- **The two miss different cases.** Of the 32 cases Opus misses in most of its runs, DepTime's first run has 28;
  of the 25 DepTime missed, Opus has 21. One or the other is right on 187 of the 191 (Sonnet: 186; Haiku: 184).
- **A model's answers are steady from run to run.** Opus gives the same mark in all three runs on 186 of the 191
  cases, Sonnet on 171, Haiku on 162, and no model's headline moves by more than 2.6 points between runs.
- **What the two say when they are wrong differs.** DepTime gave a finding for 176 of the 191 tracebacks, and 167
  of those name the right root cause (94.9%); for the other 15 it said it had none. The models were told always to
  answer, so their wrong releases come in the same form as their right ones. This run doesn't measure whether a
  model would abstain if asked to.

## What to keep in mind

- **The scoring favours the model where it could.** Either side of the boundary counts, an import name counts as
  the package, and form isn't marked (README.md). Without the first allowance ("same side only" below) each model
  would lose 7 to 20 cases a run.
- **The tracebacks are public.** They come from GitHub issues, most with the answer in the thread, so a model may
  have read the issue itself where the break is older than its training data.
- **One vendor, one effort level, no tools.** Three Claude models at effort `high`. A coding agent can also read
  the installed package's source or search the web, which this leaves out; whether an agent fixes more breakages
  with DepTime than without is DT-QA-05, and this result makes that the measure that matters.
